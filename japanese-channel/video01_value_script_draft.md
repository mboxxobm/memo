### 0:30–1:30 You're not alone (data)
**SPOKEN:**
"First, some facts. The Japan Foundation counted about **4 million people** studying Japanese at schools and institutions in **143 countries and regions** in 2024. That was the first time the number went over 4 million.
And that number *doesn't include* self-learners like you, studying with apps, YouTube, or anime. So the real number is bigger. We just don't know how much bigger.
The top countries: China, Indonesia, Korea, Australia, and Thailand."
**DATA SLIDE 1:** 4,000,750 learners (2024, institutions only) / 143 countries & regions / Top 5: China 1,019,197 · Indonesia 732,914 · Korea 555,396 · Australia 424,316 · Thailand 194,366
Source: Japan Foundation, *Survey on Japanese-Language Education Abroad 2024* — https://www.jpf.go.jp/e/project/japanese/survey/result/information/dl/overview_of_survey_results_en.pdf

### 1:30–2:30 Why people start: "I like it"
**SPOKEN:**
"Why do people start? In the same survey, the top reason schools reported was **anime, manga, J-POP, and fashion** — 61 percent of institutions said so. Next came interest in the language itself, and history and culture.
Study in Japan, work in Japan, and travel to Japan came after that.
So if you started because of anime — that's normal. That's the most common reason in the world."
**DATA SLIDE 2:** Reasons reported by institutions (multiple answers): Anime/manga/J-POP/fashion 61.3% · The Japanese language itself 56.8% · History/literature/arts 44.4% · Study in Japan 37.3% · Future work in Japan 36.4% · Sightseeing 33.9%
Footnote on screen: "% of institutions, not % of learners"
Source: same Japan Foundation PDF (section 5)

### 2:30–3:30 Why "liking it" isn't enough
**SPOKEN:**
"But here's the honest part. Liking something helps you start. It doesn't always keep you going.
The U.S. Foreign Service Institute puts Japanese in its hardest group for English speakers: about **88 weeks, or 2,200 class hours**, to reach professional working level.
That's a long road. Somewhere in the middle (for many people, around kanji or keigo), 'I like anime' isn't enough anymore.
Researchers have a name for what helps. Zoltán Dörnyei called it the **'Ideal L2 Self'**: a clear picture of *you* in the future, using the language. A meta-analysis found that this future picture is strongly linked to how much effort people *plan* to put in.
To be fair, it's only weakly linked to test scores. A picture alone won't pass N3. You also need a plan."
**ON SCREEN:** 好き ＝ start / 目的 ＝ keep going · "Ideal L2 Self = the future you who uses Japanese"
**DATA SLIDE 3:** FSI Category IV: ~88 weeks / 2,200 class hours (Japanese, Arabic, Mandarin, Cantonese, Korean) — https://www.state.gov/national-foreign-affairs-training-center/foreign-language-training
Dörnyei (2009), The L2 Motivational Self System — https://doi.org/10.21832/9781847691293-003
Al-Hoorie (2018) meta-analysis: Ideal L2 self vs intended effort r≈.61; vs achievement r≈.20 — https://doi.org/10.14746/ssllt.2018.8.4.2

### 3:30–4:45 Add ONE concrete purpose: what Japanese can open
**SPOKEN:**
"So let's add one concrete purpose. Here are some real doors Japanese can open.
One: **tests**. In 2025, about **1.65 million** people took the JLPT. That's a record.
Two: **visas**. Japan's points system for highly skilled professionals gives **15 bonus points for N1** and **10 for N2**. You need 70 points in total. Japanese alone won't get you the visa, but it helps.
Three: **jobs**. Japan had about **2.57 million foreign workers** in October 2025, the most ever.
And four, one example from my own life: **teaching or using Japanese.**
This is *my estimate*, and it's only *one example*, not a promise.
In my experience, online lessons for students overseas are around **7,800 yen per session**. At a Japanese cram school it's around **3,000 yen**. With private clients, it's about **7,800 to 8,000 yen**.
On platforms like Preply, the average listed Japanese tutor rate was about **25 to 26 US dollars an hour** when I checked.
Your results will be different. The point is this: Japanese is a skill, and skills have value."
**ON SCREEN (always visible during the money part):** ⚠ My estimate / one example — not a promise of income
**DATA SLIDE 4:** JLPT examinees: 2023 1,265,435 → 2024 1,470,989 → 2025 1,645,713 — https://www.jlpt.jp/e/statistics/archive.html
**DATA SLIDE 5:** Highly Skilled Professional points: N1 = +15, N2 = +10 (no double counting), 70 points needed — https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/content/930001657.pdf / https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/resources/newimmiact_3_qa.html
**DATA SLIDE 6:** Foreign workers in Japan: 2,571,037 (end of Oct 2025, +11.7% year on year, record) — MHLW https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/newpage_68794.html

### 4:45–6:00 How to stay motivated: review weekly, monthly, every 6 months
**SPOKEN:**
"Now, how do you keep going? Not with more willpower. With a check-in schedule.
**Every week:** five minutes. Did I study? What was hard?
**Every month:** Did I reach this month's goal? Is it too big or too small? Change it.
**Every six months:** Look at your reason again. Is it still true? Maybe you started for anime, and now you want to work in Japan. That's great. Update it.
One more trick from psychology: 'if-then' plans. Say: '*If* it's 9 p.m., *then* I do 15 minutes of kanji.' In a big review of 94 studies, plans like this helped people reach their goals much more often.
Small, fixed, and checked. That's how you keep going."
**ON SCREEN:** 毎週 Weekly 5 min / 毎月 Monthly adjust / 半年 6 months: re-check your "why" · "If ___, then I ___."
**DATA SLIDE 8:** Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006) meta-analysis, 94 tests, d = .65 — https://cancercontrol.cancer.gov/sites/default/files/2020-06/goal%5Fintent%5Fattain.pdf
